| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Goals & Alignment (Goals–Activities–Assessment)**                              | Goals are vague or the plan is mostly a list of activities. Assessment is add-on and misaligned. | Goals, activities, and assessment are present, but alignment is partial; noticeable mismatches remain.                       | Goals are stated as measurable learning outcomes; activities and assessment mostly align with the goals.                   | Goals specify clear learning outcomes (and, where relevant, broader competencies). Assessment criteria/evidence are explicit, and alignment is tight so assessment drives learning.           |
| **2. Design Grounded in Knowledge of Learners**                                     | Little/no description of learners; design rationale is generic.                                  | General assumptions about grade-level learners are provided, but limited evidence of learners’ actual prior knowledge/needs. | Anticipates likely prior knowledge and misconceptions; instructional choices are justified with reference to learners.     | Uses concrete information about learners (diagnostic data, prior lessons, observations, etc.) and leverages learners’ assets (interests/experiences) to justify design decisions.             |
| **3. Learner-Centered Deep Learning Activities (Constructive + Interactive)**       | Student work is largely passive or procedural; little opportunity for meaning-making.            | Includes some discussion/participation, but constructive thinking (explaining, reasoning) and interaction are shallow.       | Includes constructive tasks (explain, compare, justify) and productive dialogue/collaboration that supports learning.      | Meaning-making (explain, re-represent, critique, synthesize) and interaction (peer explanation, integration, peer assessment) are central and necessary for learning.                         |
| **4. Scaffolding & Cognitive Load Management**                                      | Few supports for difficult points; too much information/procedures; cognitive overload likely.   | Supports exist but are not targeted (unclear where/for whom); cognitive load is not well managed.                            | Provides appropriate scaffolds (examples, guided steps, hints) at key challenge points; sequencing is generally effective. | Scaffolds are adaptive and strategically faded (supported → independent), with alternative pathways as needed; extraneous load is minimized while focusing students on essential thinking.    |
| **5. Formative Assessment & Feedback (Eliciting Evidence → Adjusting Instruction)** | No clear formative assessment plan; “checking” is superficial and not connected to next steps.   | Some checks for understanding are planned, but what to look for/how to respond is unclear.                                   | Specifies what evidence will be collected, how it will be interpreted, and what feedback will be given.                    | Clearly plans the cycle: evidence elicitation → timely feedback → concrete in-lesson adjustments (re-teach, task change, added supports, regrouping, etc.).                                   |
| **6. Access & Participation for All (Differentiation + Motivation)**                | Participation is limited to some learners; little differentiation or access support.             | Mentions accommodations in general terms, but lacks concrete options (supports/extensions/alternatives).                     | Provides supports for struggling learners and extensions for advanced learners; offers options for demonstrating learning. | Anticipates diverse needs (struggling/advanced/special supports) with multiple access routes and evidence options; also supports autonomy, competence, and relatedness to sustain engagement. |
